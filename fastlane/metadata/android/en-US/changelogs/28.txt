- Changed: the health check now treats "any HTTP response came back" as healthy. The old rule could not tell a real 204 from a carrier's out-of-data portal page, but a portal is still proof that the data plane works — rebuilding the proxy cannot fix it, and calling it a failure only produced two pointless rebuilds plus a notification that said the opposite of what was happening. Responses that do not match the endpoint's expectation are now labelled in the diagnostics (`HTTP200(unexpected)`), so a carrier redirect stays visible without changing the restart decision
- Fixed: the health check always probed its endpoints in a fixed order, so on a network where the first endpoint is blocked it paid a full timeout on every round — thousands of times a day while the proxy sat idle. The endpoint that succeeded last time is now tried first, so a healthy network costs one request
- Fixed: redirects are no longer followed, so a portal page is not downloaded only to be discarded, and the diagnostics show the plain `HTTP302`
- Fixed: a completely failing egress probe could take over 20 seconds (two STUN plus two echo timeouts) while running every 15 seconds, which stretched its own cycle. It now runs on an 8-second budget, with timeouts clamped to whatever is left
